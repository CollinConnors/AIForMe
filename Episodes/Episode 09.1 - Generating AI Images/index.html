    Episode 08.1 Homework Problems

    081.1: Pick Your Challenges

    Background:
    In class, you were introduced to four AI image generation challenges: generating an image from a text prompt, converting a hand-drawn outline into a finished image, editing an existing image, and creating an infographic. Each challenge requires a different kind of thinking about how to communicate intent to an AI system.

    Instructions:
    Select any two of the four in-class challenges and complete them outside of class using an AI image generation tool of your choice (such as Adobe Firefly, Microsoft Designer, DALL-E, or Midjourney). For each challenge you select, your prompt should be original — do not reuse the exact prompts demonstrated in class.

    Prepare a short report that includes:

    • The two challenges you selected and why you chose them
    • The AI tool you used for each challenge and why you selected it
    • The prompt or instructions you provided to the AI for each challenge
    • The image(s) produced, included as screenshots or exported files in your report
    • A brief reflection on how well the output matched your intent and what you would change if you were to try again

    081.2: The Prompt as a Craft — Iteration and Refinement

    Background:
    A common misconception about AI image generation is that results are mostly random or unpredictable. In practice, the quality and accuracy of an output depends heavily on the structure and specificity of the prompt. In class, you were introduced to prompt elements such as subject, setting, action, composition, camera framing, lighting, mood, style, and constraints. This assignment asks you to treat prompt writing as a deliberate, iterative craft.

    Instructions:
    Choose a single image concept you want to create. Beginning with a short, vague prompt, generate an image and then progressively refine your prompt across at least four iterations, adding or adjusting one or more prompt elements each time. Document every version.

    Prepare a short report that addresses the following:

    • Your starting concept and why you chose it
    • All four prompt versions, displayed side-by-side with the image each produced. Include screenshots of both the prompt and the output for each version
    • For each iteration, explain specifically what you changed and why — what were you trying to improve or correct?
    • Which prompt elements from class (subject, lighting, mood, style, etc.) had the most noticeable impact on your results, and which had little effect?
    • A final reflection: what does this exercise reveal about the skill of prompt writing? Is it closer to writing, directing, or something else entirely?

    081.3: Bias and Representation in AI-Generated Images

    Background:
    AI image generation models are trained on large datasets scraped from the internet. Because those datasets reflect the biases present in human-created media — who is photographed, how they are portrayed, and in what contexts — the models can reproduce and sometimes amplify those biases in their outputs. Understanding this is essential for anyone who uses these tools to create content that represents people.

    Instructions:
    Select three occupations or social roles (for example: nurse, engineer, criminal, CEO, teacher, or athlete). For each one, generate the same prompt at least six times using a tool that does not require you to specify gender, age, or ethnicity (e.g., simply "a photo of a nurse"). Record all outputs.

    Prepare a short report that addresses the following:

    • The three roles you selected and the exact prompt you used for each
    • A summary of the patterns you observed across your generated images — who was depicted and who was not, in terms of apparent gender, age, ethnicity, and setting. Include a sample of your outputs as screenshots
    • Search for at least one published article or research study on bias in AI image generation and summarize its key findings. Include a reference to the source
    • How do your findings compare to what the research describes?
    • Try modifying your prompts to produce a more diverse set of outputs. What changes worked, and what does that tell you about how these models respond to explicit versus implicit instructions?
    • Conclude with a reflection: what responsibility do users, developers, and platforms share for the representations that AI image tools produce?

    081.4: The Limits of the Machine

    Background:
    AI image generation tools are impressive, but they are not capable of understanding the world the way humans do. They work by learning statistical relationships between text and images in their training data. This means they can struggle with abstract concepts, precise spatial relationships, factual accuracy, and anything that falls outside what their training data covered well. Studying where and why these tools fail is one of the most effective ways to understand how they actually work.

    Instructions:
    Design a set of three image prompts specifically intended to challenge or confuse an AI image generation tool. Image generation tools tend to be poor at abstract or philosophical concepts (e.g., irony, justice, entropy), precise spatial or numerical instructions (e.g., exactly four red apples arranged in a triangle), factual or historical accuracy (e.g., a specific real-world event depicted correctly), visual paradoxes or impossible scenes, and emotionally complex or culturally specific scenarios.

    Prepare a short report that addresses the following:

    • Your three prompts, the category each belongs to, and why you expected each to be difficult
    • The output for each prompt, included as screenshots, and an assessment of whether and how the model failed
    • Were any results surprisingly successful? If so, what might explain that?
    • Conclude with a reflection: based on what you observed, what kinds of tasks are AI image tools genuinely well-suited for, and what kinds of tasks should still be left to humans?

    081.5: Real-World Brief — Creating Images for a Client

    Background:
    Generating an image that looks interesting is very different from generating an image that solves a real communication problem. Professional designers, marketers, and communicators must create visuals that meet specific audience needs, match a brand's tone, and serve a clear purpose. This assignment asks you to apply your image generation skills within the constraints of a realistic brief, and to articulate the reasoning behind your creative choices.

    Instructions:
    You are working as a creative consultant for a local non-profit organization of your choosing (for example, an animal shelter, a food bank, a youth mentoring program, or an environmental group). The organization needs three images for an upcoming social media campaign aimed at recruiting volunteers. You must define the audience, tone, and message yourself, as you would if a real client had given you only a general goal.

    Prepare a short report that includes:

    • A one-paragraph description of the organization you selected, the target audience for the campaign, and the overall tone and message you decided on
    • Your three final images, each accompanied by the prompt used to generate it and a client-facing rationale (two to three sentences explaining why this image fits the brief — write this as if you are presenting to the organization's director)
    • A description of your process: how many versions did you generate before settling on each final image? What did you reject and why?
    • A reflection on the experience of working within constraints: how did having a specific audience, purpose, and tone change the way you wrote your prompts compared to open-ended image generation?
    • Finally, consider the ethical dimension: are there any concerns about using AI-generated images to represent a real community or cause? What obligations, if any, does the creator have?
