    # Episode 10.1 Homework Problems

    ## 101.1 — Credit, Authorship, and the “Nobel Problem”

    **Background:**
    The lecture raises a core question in AI-enabled discovery: “If AI does it, who deserves the Nobel prize?”
    
    **Instructions:**
    Choose one AI-in-science use case (examples from the slides include medical imaging triage, climate/materials, AlphaFold, astronomy, mental health diagnosis but you can pick other areas as well). 

    Write a short report that answers:

    1. Who should get credit and why?
    Consider roles such as:

        * AI developers / model builders
        * domain scientists who framed the question
        * data creators/curators (lab techs, annotators, instrumentation teams)
        * validation/verification team (experiments, clinicians, peer review)

    2. Propose a clear rule for credit/authorship (e.g., a checklist or weighted scoring system).

    3. Describe one downside of your rule (how it could be gamed, who it might disadvantage, or what it fails to capture).

    ## 101.2 — Should AI Diagnose Patients?

    **Background:**
    The lecture highlights AI in healthcare imaging (e.g., chest X-ray triage tools) and asks directly: **“Should AI diagnose patients?”**  
    This is not only a technical question—it’s about risk, accountability, and human oversight.

    **Instructions:**
    Assume a hospital wants to deploy an AI system that analyzes medical images and produces a diagnosis suggestion or triage score.

    Design a short protocol that answers:

    1. **Scope of autonomy:** What can AI do on its own vs what requires clinician sign-off?
    2. **Evidence threshold:** What must be true before deployment (validation, performance targets, monitoring plan)?
    3. **Disagreement handling:** What happens if AI and clinician disagree? (Escalation path.)
    4. **Accountability:** Who is responsible if harm occurs (vendor, hospital, clinician, regulator)? Defend your view.
    5. **Failure modes:** Address at least two concrete failure modes (e.g., bias across populations, dataset shift, automation bias, false negatives).

    ## 091.3 — If AI Can Do the Science, What Is the Human Scientist’s Role?

    **Background:**
    The lecture asks: **“If AI can generate hypotheses, analyze data, and write results, what is the value of human scientists?”** 
    It also prompts: **“How do we use AI effectively in science?”** 

    **Instructions:**
    Write a response in **two parts**:

    **Part A — Where AI adds real scientific value**
    Identify **three** tasks where AI can meaningfully help science (not just “it’s faster”). For each:

    * describe *what the AI does*
    * describe *why that helps scientifically* (e.g., pattern discovery, search, measurement, prediction, uncertainty estimates, automation of routine analysis)

    **Part B — What must remain human-led**
    Identify **three** responsibilities that should remain primarily human-led. For each:

    * explain why humans must lead it (ethics, meaning-making, accountability, choice of what questions matter, etc.)
    * include one example of what could go wrong if humans fully outsource it

    **Finish with one concrete recommendation:**
    Write **5 “rules”** for how a college student should use AI effectively in science (e.g., for writing, data analysis, study, lab work) without outsourcing understanding.
